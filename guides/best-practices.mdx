Power users across industries have refined the way they work in the app over time, and the patterns that emerge are remarkably consistent. Whether you're just getting started or looking to level up an existing workflow, the practices below will help you stay organized, move faster, and get more out of every session.

## Organize Your Workspace

A well-structured workspace reduces friction for you and everyone on your team. Invest a little time upfront and you'll spend far less time searching later.

**Use consistent naming conventions.** Decide on a format for naming records, projects, and folders — then stick to it. For example, prefix project names with a year or department code (`2025-Marketing-Q3`) so they sort predictably and remain scannable at a glance.

**Create a clear folder hierarchy.** Limit nesting to two or three levels. Deep hierarchies are hard to navigate and easy to lose things in. A flat-but-tagged structure often scales better as your account grows.

**Apply tags liberally.** Tags are the fastest way to cross-reference items that live in different folders. Create a shared tag vocabulary for your team (e.g., `urgent`, `blocked`, `review-needed`) and document it somewhere visible so everyone uses the same labels.

## Work Efficiently

Small efficiency gains compound quickly when you're using the app every day. Learn the shortcuts that match your most frequent actions.

**Keyboard shortcuts** eliminate the need to reach for your mouse on common operations. Here are the most frequently used:

```
?              Open keyboard shortcut reference
N              Create new record
/              Focus global search
E              Edit selected record
⌘ + Enter      Save and close (Mac) / Ctrl + Enter (Windows)
⌘ + K          Open command palette
⌘ + Z          Undo last action
⌘ + Shift + D  Duplicate selected record
```

**Use batch actions** when you need to update multiple records at once. Select several items using the checkbox column, then use the bulk-action toolbar that appears to reassign, retag, archive, or delete them all in a single step. Batch actions can save hours compared to editing records one at a time.

**Pin your most-used views** to the sidebar so they're always one click away. If you find yourself navigating to the same filtered view repeatedly, pin it rather than recreating the filter each time.

## Collaborate Effectively

Collaboration works best when communication happens in context — right next to the record it relates to — rather than scattered across emails or chat threads.

**Leave comments on records, not in chat.** When feedback, decisions, or questions are attached directly to the relevant record, anyone who opens that record later has full context. Chat messages get lost; comments don't.

**Use @mentions to route information.** Typing `@` followed by a teammate's name sends them a notification and creates a clear audit trail of who was informed. Reserve @mentions for genuinely actionable requests — over-mentioning leads to alert fatigue.

**Keep status fields up to date.** Status fields are the heartbeat of shared workflows. When you move a record from `In Progress` to `Ready for Review`, teammates and automations alike react to that signal. Stale statuses undermine trust in the system.

## Keep Your Data Clean

Over time, every account accumulates records that are no longer active. A regular cleanup routine keeps the workspace fast and the signal-to-noise ratio high.

**Archive instead of delete.** Archiving removes a record from your active views without permanently destroying it. Prefer archiving for anything you might need to reference later — closed deals, completed projects, or deprecated templates. Delete only when you're certain the record has no future value.

**Run a monthly cleanup pass.** Set a recurring calendar reminder to review records that haven't been updated in 60 or more days. Archive anything stale, resolve any open comments, and close out tasks that have been sitting in `In Progress` long past their due date.

**Archive old projects before starting new ones.** Before kicking off a new initiative, archive the previous one. This keeps your active workspace focused and prevents old records from cluttering search results or filter views.

## Use Automations

Automations handle the repetitive, rule-based work so you don't have to. If you find yourself doing the same manual update more than a few times a week, it's a strong signal that an automation could own that task.

Start with the highest-frequency, lowest-complexity actions: status-change notifications, due-date reminders, and auto-assignment rules. These deliver immediate value and are easy to test and adjust. Once you're comfortable with the basics, explore more advanced multi-step workflows.

For a full walkthrough of setting up automation rules, see the [Automations guide](/guides/automations).

<Tip>
  **Schedule a weekly 15-minute review.** Every week, spend a few minutes scanning your workspace: archive completed work, update stale statuses, and triage anything that slipped through the cracks. Teams that build this habit consistently report cleaner data and fewer "where did that go?" moments.
</Tip>
